READING THE END-TO-END MAIN PROOF

This is a navigation guide, not an independent mathematical proof check.
Every path below is relative to the project root. Fully qualified names can
be searched in docs/DECLARATIONS.html or inspected in Lean's InfoView.

1. READ THE FINAL STATEMENT AND ITS PREMISE

Open Junpei/RealPrecisionMain.lean:
  Junpei.RealPrecisionMain.InitialHistoryRiskOptimal
  Junpei.RealPrecisionMain.theorem2_paper_exponent
  Junpei.RealPrecisionMain.theorem2_exponential_rate
  Junpei.RealPrecisionMain.exists_initialHistoryRiskOptimal

The policy is a Borel randomized posterior-state algorithm. The optimality
premise compares its initial actual posterior risk with every concrete Borel
full-history competitor, for every initialized posterior mean vector.
The theorem uses true means (0,0,Delta), Delta>0, and sufficiently large odd T.
The explicit exponent is (33+Delta)^2/2+4. The existence theorem establishes
that the policy premise has witnesses.

Read the definition before the proof: in Lean, the colon after a theorem name
introduces the proposition and all arguments before it are hypotheses or
parameters. The expression after `:= by` is the proof checked by Lean.
Definitions specify objects; they do not by themselves prove claims about them.

4. UNDERSTAND THE BAYES-OPTIMALITY FOUNDATION

Start with Junpei/GeneralOptimalityFinite.lean for Theorem 1:
  Junpei.GeneralOptimality.FiniteHorizon.Policy
  Junpei.GeneralOptimality.FiniteHorizon.SequentiallyOptimal
  Junpei.GeneralOptimality.FiniteHorizon.theorem1
  Junpei.GeneralOptimality.FiniteHorizon.exists_sequentiallyOptimal

The policy has exactly N Borel sampling rules and one terminal rule, defined
only on proper Gaussian states. For the manuscript, N=T-K and sampling index
j=T-t records the number of draws remaining after round t. The hypothesis
minimizes actual posterior risk against every concrete Borel history policy,
at every proper state and every remaining horizon within this fixed budget.
It contains no Bellman-action premise. The theorem derives the Bellman risk
identity, EBI-maximizing sampling, and maximum-mean terminal recommendation.
The decision rules hold almost surely in the fresh standard normal randomizer.

Revision 2 adds the arbitrary-policy implication at this generality. The first
release had a general finite-arm constructed optimum, but its arbitrary-policy
necessity result used three arms and integer counts. Follow the new proof via:

  Junpei.GeneralOptimality.toRaw_compile / actualRisk_compile
    Compile a real-precision state policy into a concrete history competitor,
    proving exact raw-strategy and actual-risk identities.

  Junpei.GeneralOptimality.minimizing_actualRisk_eq_loss
  Junpei.GeneralOptimality.minimizing_draw_EBI_ae / minimizing_recommend_ae
    Compare with the compiled canonical competitor and derive the necessary
    decision rules from single-state actual-risk minimization.

  GeneralOptimalityProper / GeneralOptimalityFinite
    Extend a proper-domain policy to the existing interpreter and pad a finite
    rule collection only outside its budget. Direct interpreters and proved
    raw-tree/risk equalities show that these constructions preserve the paper
    policy's execution. No extra domain or larger-horizon optimality is assumed.

FiniteGaussian / FinitePosterior provide the Bellman model and optimality proof
for an arbitrary finite nonempty arm set with proper Gaussian posteriors.
ObservationBridge derives the observation/update identity from the joint
Gaussian law. EmbeddingExecution / EmbeddingRandomized establish the actual
raw-observation risk recursion and integrability. JointGaussianMeasurable and
RealPrecisionPolicy give jointly Borel selectors and a globally defined policy.

FlatPriorInitialization / FlatPriorProduct / AdaptivePosteriorSufficiency /
FixedGaussianFlatInitialization connect the initialized flat likelihood to the
proper Gaussian continuation laws. The labelled-likelihood statement should
not be read as an independent regular-conditional-law theorem for an arbitrary
adaptive history; consult ALL_ARGUMENTS_STATUS.txt for the precise boundary.

6. DISTINGUISH THE ORIGINAL FOOTNOTE FROM ITS CORRECTED STATEMENT

Keep the negative result separate when presenting this work: the original
Footnote 14 counterexample refutes the manuscript's claimed necessary scale.
It is not a proof of the proposed Appendix F event improvement.

Revision 3 adds Junpei/Footnote14Characterization.lean. Start with
  Junpei.Footnote14Characterization.corrected_footnote14_iff.
For an arbitrary nonnegative sequence d, it proves that a lower bound
P[N(0,T^-3)>=d(T)] >= c*T^(-p), with positive real c,p and a fixed natural
T0 for all T>=T0, is equivalent to d(T)=O(T^(-3/2)*sqrt(log T)). The constants
are independent of T and no monotonicity of d is required. The probability
uses the actual Gaussian measure; the asymptotic relation is Lean's IsBigO.

The two implications are eventuallyPolynomialTail_of_isBigO and
isBigO_of_eventuallyPolynomialTail in the same namespace. The theorem
realExponentPolynomialTail_iff_eventually converts the eventual natural-
exponent predicate to the explicit positive-real-exponent statement above.
tail_standardize identifies the probability with the standard Gaussian tail.
These are proof dependencies checked by Lean. The original counterexample
is retained, and the supplied PDF is unchanged.
