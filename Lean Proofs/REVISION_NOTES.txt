Exact requested endpoint:
  Junpei.Footnote14Characterization.corrected_footnote14_iff

For any d : Nat -> Real with forall T, 0 <= d T, this states the equivalence:
  there exist real c > 0, real p > 0, and natural T0 such that for all T >= T0,
  c*T^(-p) <= (gaussianReal 0 (((T : NNReal)^3)^(-1))).real (Ici (d T));
  if and only if d =O[atTop] (fun T => T^(-3/2)*sqrt(log T)).

The quantifier order makes c, p, and T0 independent of T, while allowing them
to depend on d. No monotonicity, continuity, convergence, or other assumptions
on d are present. The tail is the actual Gaussian probability, with variance
T^(-3); the right side uses Lean's asymptotic notation.

Proof route:
  tail_standardize uses the existing standardDeviation and its squared-scale
  identity to identify the Gaussian tail with the standard-normal tail.
  eventuallyPolynomialTail_of_isBigO proves sufficiency using the existing
  Gaussian polynomial lower bound and monotonicity of the tail event.
  standardized_threshold_sq_bound and isBigO_of_eventuallyPolynomialTail
  prove necessity using existing Gaussian logarithmic tail estimates.
  realExponentPolynomialTail_iff_eventually proves both conversions between
  the intermediate natural exponent and the exact positive real exponent and
  explicit starting horizon used in the final statement.

The proof uses these already formalized tail estimates; the optional new
density-interval estimate suggested in the request is not needed as a lemma.
All parts of the requested equivalence are proved in the final endpoint.

REVISION 2 BACKGROUND: THEOREM 1 CORRECTION

The first release overstated the scope of its Theorem 1 coverage. It proved
that a constructed policy is optimal for general finite arm sets, but its
implication from arbitrary policy optimality to the stated sampling and
recommendation rules was restricted to three arms and integer counts.
Repackaging those same proofs did not establish the missing general result.

Revision 2 added four modules and 35 public theorem declarations. Its complete
inventory was 155 proof modules and 1,505 public theorem/lemma declarations.
The original 151 proof modules are unchanged; their proofs remain dependencies.

NEW PROOF CHAIN

  Junpei/GeneralOptimalityHistory.lean (6 theorems)
    Compile posterior-state rules into concrete Borel history policies and

  Junpei/GeneralOptimality.lean (10 theorems)
    Derive Bellman risk equality, EBI-maximizing draws, and maximal-mean
    recommendations from actual-risk minimization at a single state. Package
    the conclusions for every remaining horizon of a fixed finite budget.

  Junpei/GeneralOptimalityProper.lean (8 theorems)
    Use rules defined only on proper Gaussian states. Prove that extending
    those rules to unused improper states preserves their direct execution
    and actual risk; transfer the characterization to the original domain.

  Junpei/GeneralOptimalityFinite.lean (11 theorems)
    Use exactly N sampling rules, indexed by Fin N, and one terminal rule.
    Prove that constant padding outside the budget preserves execution and
    risk. Establish the characterization and a concrete optimal-policy witness
    in this finite class. No rules beyond the fixed budget are hypotheses.

PRINCIPAL THEOREM 1 ENDPOINT

  Junpei.GeneralOptimality.FiniteHorizon.theorem1

The policy is any Borel randomized posterior-state policy in the finite class.
Its premise is actual posterior-risk minimization against all concrete Borel
history continuation policies at each proper state and remaining horizon.
The arm set is an arbitrary finite nonempty ordered type, including Fin K for
every K >= 1. All posterior precisions are arbitrary strictly positive reals.
For the paper, N = T - K and sampling index j = T - t.

The theorem derives all three conclusions: actual risk equals Bellman loss;
the selected arm maximizes EBI almost surely in the fresh Gaussian randomizer;
and the terminal recommendation maximizes posterior mean almost surely.
The conclusions are not assumptions in the actual-risk optimality predicate.
The companion exists_sequentiallyOptimal theorem supplies a concrete witness.
